Communication Skills

1What is communication?

The word ‘communication’ comes from the Latin word commūnicāre, meaning ‘to share’. Communication is the ‘sharing’ of information between two or more individuals or within the group to reach a common understanding.

2What is the importance of communication?

Communication helps to improve physical, social and good relationships between family and friends.

Three importance of communication in day-to-day life

Communication occupies a central place in our everyday working and personal life. The three uses below are the ones an employer looks for first.

🗣️ Inform

We give facts or information to someone in our daily life.

🧲 Influence

Sometimes we are required to influence or change someone in an indirect but usually important way.

❤️ Express feelings

If you are expressing your views or feelings to others, it is a healthy way to express them.

3What are the elements of communication?

Communication means giving and receiving, which is a two-way exchange of information.

Giving information→Speaking & Writing Receiving information→Reading & Listening

Giving information is an example of speaking and writing, whereas receiving information is an example of reading and listening.

4What are perspectives in communication?

Perspectives are ideas, views, or fixed ways of thinking. These sometimes affect our communication. For example, if you have a fixed idea that your teacher or father is strict, even when they are being friendly, you may think they are scolding you.

In the same way, others may also have fixed ideas about you, which affects whatever you say to them.

5Factors affecting perspectives in communication

Sometimes, we are not able to communicate clearly because of barriers that stop us from sharing and understanding messages.

6What is effective communication? (The 7 C's)

There are 7 C’s of effective communication which are applicable for both written as well as oral communication. These are as follows —

1 · Clear

Always say clearly what you want to say.

2 · Concise

Always use simple language and say only what is required.

3 · Concrete

Always use proper words and phrases in the sentences.

4 · Correct

Always use correct spelling and grammar in the sentences.

5 · Coherent

Your words should be related to the main topic and your words should make sense.

6 · Complete

Your message should be complete and have all the needed information.

7 · Courteous

Be honest, respectful and friendly with others.

✅ Advantages of Verbal communication

  • Time Saving – We can express our thoughts easily and quickly and it is an easier form of communication.
  • Quick Feedback – We can get a quick response from the listener.

⛔ Disadvantages of Verbal communication

  • Cultural Difference – The cultural difference is the main disadvantage of verbal communication.
  • Language barriers – Difficult to share thoughts or ideas in different languages.

9What is public speaking?

Speaking face-to-face to a live audience or in front of a large group is known as public speaking. You can use the 3Ps method to get over your fears and become a confident speaker.

1 · Prepare→2 · Practice→3 · Perform

📌 Note: The 3Ps method is used to overcome stage fear — first you prepare your content, then you practice it (aloud, in front of a mirror or a friend), and finally you perform it before the audience.

Without using words, if you are sending some information to others it is known as non-verbal communication. Some of the non-verbal communication is —

👁️ Eye contact

11Importance of non-verbal communication?

In our daily life it is observed that most of the communication is done using body movements and voice control.

  • In non-verbal communication you can make your message stronger as compared to written communication.
  • Helps overcome cultural barriers.
  • With the right gestures while speaking makes our message more effective.
  • Using the right gestures and postures helps us to be professional at work.
  • The message is delivered and communicated more effectively than verbal communication.
  • In the time of public speech you can understand the audience’s reaction easily and adjust our behaviour or communication accordingly.

12Types of non-verbal communication

We communicate non-verbally in many ways —

😊 Facial expressions

The movement and changes of the face are the facial expressions. Many times facial expressions show the feelings of a person. For example, expressing your thoughts using a smile — when we are happy, or when we are sad.

🧍 Posture

Posture means body positions. Posture shows how confident you are and your emotional feelings. For example, a straight body posture means confidence.

✋ Gestures / Body language

Gestures mean if you are sharing any idea or meaning with the help of your hands or head. For example, pointing, waving and using our hands when speaking.

🤝 Touch

Sometimes we communicate using touch; we also share messages with others through it, for example handshaking and patting on the back.

↔️ Space

When two people are communicating you will find some space between these two people depending on the closeness or intimacy between them.

👁️ Eye contact

Maintaining eye contact is very important when you are talking about interest, whereas looking at any other side can make the other person feel ignored.

🎚️ Paralanguage

The tone of our voice, speed and volume that makes a difference in the meaning is a paralanguage.

13What is visual communication?

In daily life we are using visual communication. Sometimes we are getting messages from images or pictures. The main advantage of visual communication is that you do not need to know any particular language.

14What is assertive communication?

Being assertive is a better way to communicate. It involves —

  • expressing your thoughts without hurting others or yourself,
  • respecting others,
  • expressing yourself and your goals in a clear and concise manner,
  • being receptive to other people’s opinions, and
  • finding a balance between your needs and the needs of others.

📌 Remember: the original notes print this line as “Being aggressive is a better way to communicate”, but the five points that follow describe assertive behaviour — expressing yourself without hurting anyone. Aggressive = hurting others · Passive = hurting yourself · Assertive = respecting both.

Strength and Weakness Analysis

Q. Self-management skills — strengths and weaknesses?

💪 Strengths

  1. Think that you are always good at.
  2. Think about what you do well.
  3. Think about what others appreciate about you.

🔧 Weaknesses

  1. Identify the areas where you struggle and what you find difficult to do.
  2. Accept the feedback you receive from others.
  3. Be positive with feedback and try to improve yourself.

Difference between interests and abilities

Q. Difference between interests and abilities?

Interests

Interests are the things that we enjoy doing.

Ability

An ability is an acquired or natural capacity that enables an individual to perform a particular task with considerable proficiency.

Interest may include —

  • Activities you like most to do at school or at home that make you happy.
  • Even if no one asks you to do the activity, you want to do it.
  • Activities that you like to learn or do in the future.

Dressing

The act of putting on clothes is known as dressing.

Grooming

Benefits of dressing and grooming in daily life —

1️⃣ Look smart

We look smart and presentable.

2️⃣ Good impression

Make a good impression of ourselves on others.

3️⃣ Confidence

Feel confident about ourselves.

Personal hygiene is very important in our daily life because it helps us —

  1. Create a good image of ourselves.
  2. Stay healthy.
  3. Avoid feeling ashamed in public due to our bad breath, body odour, etc.

What are the benefits of Teamwork?

Q. What are the benefits of Teamwork?

The benefits of teamwork skill education courses are —

  1. In a team, everyone has a role to play, so the pressure to succeed does not fall only on one person.
  2. It is beneficial to have a support system since all team members help in the correction of any mistake done by one team member.
  3. When the team succeeds, it gives you a nice feeling and boosts your confidence.
  4. The work gets done faster.

📌 A team is a group of people working together towards achieving a common goal (see MCQ 15).

Networking Skills — benefits and how to build them

Q. What are the benefits of Networking Skills?

Networking supports you in making dependable and trustworthy individuals with whom you can share your life.

  • Share expertise and skills.
  • Seek and provide support in difficult times.
  • Keep up to date on fresh opportunities by informing and being informed.

Q. How to build Networking Skills?

There are six steps to develop networking skills —

  1. Converse – Give a smile and look at the person with confidence.
  2. Open up – Show interest in getting to know them.
  3. Network smartly – Talk to them about things that are common between the two.
  4. Engage – Share useful information.
  5. Communicate regularly – Stay in touch with others.
  6. Tell – Share information on events or activities.

Motivation — types, qualities and how to build it

Q. Types of Motivation?

There are two types of motivation:

🔥 Internal Motivation

Many things we do which make us happy, feel good and healthy.

🏅 External Motivation

If you are working for recognition, respect and appreciation.

Q. What are the qualities of self-motivated people?

  • Know what they want from life
  • Are focused
  • Know what is important
  • Are dedicated to fulfil their dreams

Q. How to build Self-Motivation?

There are four steps to build self-motivation —

  1. Find out your strength
  2. Set and focus on your goals
  3. Develop a plan to achieve your goals
  4. Stay loyal to your goals

How to set goals in Self-Management Skills? S · M · A · R · T

We can use different techniques to set goals.

S — Specific

Always set your clear and specific goals.

M — Measurable

A measurable goal answers the questions “How much?”, “How many?” and “How do I know that I have achieved results?”

A — Achievable

Breaking down big goals into smaller parts will make the goal achievable.

R — Realistic

A realistic goal would be something that we want to achieve and can work towards.

T — Time bound

A goal should have a timeframe by when the goal needs to be achieved.

What is time management and how can you manage your time?

The ability to organize and control how you spend the hours of your day so that you may achieve what you desire, is known as time management.

To manage effective time management, the following points are used —

  • Any planned action should not be delayed or postponed.
  • Organize your room and school desk.
  • Create a ‘NO DISTURBANCE ZONE’ where you may sit and finish important tasks without being disturbed.
  • Use waiting time productively.
  • Prepare a ‘to-do’ list.
  • Prioritize.
  • Replace useless activities with productive activities.

Terms to remember

Data

Data refers to raw facts or figures.

Information

Processed data is called Information.

Program

A set of instructions provided to the computer for doing a task is called a program.

MHz / GHz

Speed of processors is usually measured in megahertz (MHz) – millions of instructions per second; and gigahertz (GHz) – billions of instructions per second, is indicative of its power.

Baud

It is a common unit of measurement. It refers to the speed of communication over a data channel (or the speed with which information can be sent to and from a computer).

Definition · Hardware & Software · IPO Cycle

💡 A computer is an electronic device that accepts input, processes it, and displays output.

Hardware & Software: Computers work in combination with hardware and software.

🔩 Hardware

The physical electronic component of a computer is called Hardware.

Examples: Keyboard, Monitor, Speakers, Mouse, Motherboard

💿 Software

The recorded instruction/program present inside the computer is called Software.

Examples: MS-Office, Windows

IPO Cycle: Any instruction given to the computer is executed in the form of an IPO cycle.

I · Input→P · Processing→O · Output
  • Input is the process of feeding data inside the computer.
  • Processing is the process of converting input to output based on the instructions given by the user.
  • Output is the process of displaying results to the user.

Characteristics of a Computer

⚡ Speed

It is the ability of the computer to process data and instructions at very high speed. A typical high-speed computer performs 3 to 4 million instructions per second.

🎯 Versatility

It is the ability of the computer to perform multiple tasks at the same time.

✅ Accuracy

It performs 100% error-free calculations. The errors, if any, found in the output are either due to wrong entry of values or wrong instructions for computation. This phenomenon is often referred to as GIGO (Garbage In, Garbage Out).

😤 Diligence

It is the ability to perform tasks with the same speed and accuracy and does not suffer from human traits of tiredness and boredom.

🧠 Memory

It is the ability to store information for processing/future purposes.

🤖 Intelligence

Computers were merely dumb machines programmed to perform certain tasks. With the advent of artificial intelligence techniques, we now have machines that mimic human traits of decision making.

Components of a Computer — Input Unit

Input unit accepts input and is formed by the input devices attached to the computer.

⌨️ Keyboard

A device that helps the user to feed data, digits, commands etc.

🖱️ Mouse

A pointing device that helps the user to click and choose.

🎙️ Microphone

Microphone accepts sound in the form of analog signals, converts it to digital signals so that the computer can understand.

📄 Scanner

A scanner is an input device that scans documents such as photographs and pages of text. It creates an electronic version of the scanned document.

🛒 Barcode reader

A barcode reader is an optical scanner that can read printed barcodes, decode the data contained in the barcode and send the data to a computer. The barcode uses lasers to identify the position and thickness of the bars.

🖊️ Light Pen

A light-sensitive input device shaped like a pen allows the user to point to displayed objects or draw on the computer screen.

Output Unit & the CPU

Output unit displays output and is formed by the output devices attached to the computer.

🖥️ Monitor

It displays the output to the user. The picture on a monitor is made up of thousands of tiny coloured dots called pixels.

🖨️ Printer

The output is obtained as a hard copy, printed as characters/pictures on paper.

🔊 Speaker

It converts the digital signals to analog form, in a format audible to the user.

The CPU: The Central Processing Unit is also called the brain of the computer. It converts input into output based on the instructions given by the user.

🧮 Arithmetic Logic Unit (ALU)

It performs arithmetic calculations and logical operations on the given data.

🎛️ Control Unit (CU)

Control Unit acts as a supervisor as it controls and guides the interpretation, flow and manipulation of data and information. Another important function of CU is program execution.

Primary Memory is the main memory of the computer that holds data and instructions during processing. It can be divided into two parts: RAM and ROM.

🟦 Random Access Memory (RAM)

  • It is volatile in nature — the contents of RAM get erased when the power is switched off.
  • In RAM, Read/Write operations can be performed.
  • RAM holds information of the currently opened applications.
  • Normally PCs have around 8–32 GB RAM.

🟥 Read Only Memory (ROM)

  • It is non-volatile in nature — the contents remain as such even when the power is switched off.
  • In ROM, only Read operation can be performed.
  • ROM holds instructions to load the Operating System. ROM holds instructions essential for the basic input/output system (BIOS), boot up.

RAM is of two types:

Dynamic RAM (DRAM)

  • Consists of a transistor and capacitor that store binary information.
  • It is used in main memory.
  • Inexpensive.
  • Uses less power.

Static RAM (SRAM)

  • Consists of flip-flops that store binary information.
  • It is used in cache memory.
  • Expensive.
  • Consumes more power than DRAM.

Types of ROM:

PROM

Programmable read-only memory (PROM), or one-time programmable ROM (OTP), can be written or programmed via a special device called a PROM programmer. Once the chip has been programmed, the recorded information cannot be changed.

EPROM

Erasable programmable read-only memory (EPROM) can be erased by exposure to strong ultraviolet light (typically for 10 minutes or longer) and can be reprogrammed to record different information using a special PROM program facility.

EEPROM

Electrically Erasable programmable read-only memory (EEPROM) is a type of ROM that can be programmed and erased by electrical signals. One of the new applications is the use of EEPROM as a backup to RAM memory whose contents are lost in a power failure. When power is returned, EEPROM memory can be used to replace the lost contents of the RAM memory and continue to work.

🧠 Points to remember: More the RAM, more will be the space for the programs leading to faster execution.

Cache Memory & Secondary Storage

Cache memory: Cache memory is a high-speed memory that acts as a buffer between RAM and the CPU. It holds frequently requested data and instructions so that they are immediately available to the CPU when needed. Cache memory is used to reduce the average time to access data from the main memory.

Secondary storage: A secondary storage device is non-volatile in nature — the contents remain as such even when the power is switched off. It is permanent memory that stores data which can be retrieved for future use.

💽 Hard disk

Hard disk stores information on one or more circular platters (disks) coated with magnetic material. The disks are stacked with space between them. Information is recorded on the surface of rotating disks by a magnetic head as tiny magnetic spots. Storage capacity is measured in gigabytes to terabytes.

💿 CD

The Compact Disc is an optical media, in which the information is stored and retrieved with the help of light. Storage capacity is up to 700 MB of data.

📀 DVD

DVD is an optical storage device and can hold 4.7 GB (single layer, single-sided) up to 17 GB (dual layer, double-sided) of data. It is otherwise called Super Density Disc.

🔌 Flash Memory

A pen drive is a portable storage device with solid-state memory, i.e. it has no moving parts unlike magnetic/optical media. It can be accessed by inserting it in the USB (Universal Serial Bus) port. Storage capacity goes up to 128 GB and beyond.

🚀 SSD

A Solid-State Drive (SSD) is a new generation storage device used in place of hard disks. There are no moving parts present in the SSD and it keeps the data safe.

Advantages: The SSD is lighter than a HDD and is resistant to shock and temperature changes · SSD produces less noise/heat because it uses computer chips and not moving parts · A solid-state drive consumes less power when compared to an HDD.

🎬 Blue-ray Disk (BD)

It is an optical storage device that stores huge amounts of data/high-definition video. The read/write operation is performed with a blue-violet laser. The storage capacity goes up to 128 GB of data.

Advantage of BD over CD/DVD: The BD uses a blue-violet laser (405 nm) that has a shorter wavelength compared to the red laser (650 nm) used in CD/DVD. Hence in BD it is possible to focus the laser spot with even greater precision, and it allows data to be packed more tightly in less space.

Motherboard, System Bus, PSU & other devices

🧩 Motherboard

This is the main circuit board which holds together various components like CPU, memory, connectors for the hard drive and optical drives, expansion cards to control the video and audio, and connections in the form of various ports (such as USB ports). It provides connection to every component of the computer.

🛣️ System bus

System bus is an electronic pathway of connecting cables that transmits data and instructions to the major components of a computer system.

🔌 Power Supply Unit

Power Supply Unit converts alternate current power supply received by homes or offices to the low voltage direct current required by the machine.

📷 Web camera

It is an input device that captures data in the form of video/image.

📐 Plotter

A plotter is a large printer-like device that is used to take print of large maps and architectural designs. A plotter produces output size that varies from 11 to 42 inches.

📽️ Projector

A projector is an output device that displays an enlarged view of the output on a large screen.

Software · System Software · Operating System

The recorded instruction/program present inside the computer is called Software. Types of software: (a) System Software (b) Application Software.

a. System Software — The software that controls/coordinates internal computer operations (reading data from input devices, transmitting processed information to the output devices, checking system components, converting data/instructions to computer readable form) is called System Software. It can be classified into: Operating System, Language Processor, Device Driver and Utilities.

Operating System (OS): It acts as an interface between the user and the computer and plays a major role in program execution.

🔗 Communication Manager

Manages the communication needs of the system with the peripheral devices or the internet using special programs called drivers, which enable recognition of these devices and their properties.

📦 Resource Management

The operating system handles the allocation of resources (memory, CPU time, files, secondary storage, input/output devices etc.) to the various processes to get an optimum performance from the system.

⚙️ Process Management

A process is a program currently getting executed in the memory or waiting for the CPU. The OS controls and manages multiple processes being executed in the computer.

🧠 Memory Management

The memory management component allocates Random Access Memory to the processes in a dynamic manner, that is, allocated on demand and released when not needed.

🗂️ File Management

Operating system takes care of all the files and folders (directories) maintained on the computer disk. All the files stored in a computer system can be located through the file system.

Two main types of file system are:

FAT — File Allocation Table

Various FAT systems are named based on the number of bits used to store the data of FAT. For example: FAT16 uses 16 bits to store data, FAT32 uses 32 bits.

NTFS — New Technology File System

The file system used by modern Windows systems; supports large volumes, security permissions and journaling.

Types of Operating System — operating systems can be classified in different ways, depending on various parameters:

Single-tasking & Multi-tasking

A single-task OS allows execution of only a single program at any given instant. A multi-tasking OS can execute more than one program simultaneously; the processor time, in this case, is divided amongst various processes.

Single-user & Multi-user

Single-user operating systems allow only one user to use the system. Desktop systems can be classified as typical single-user systems. Multi-user operating systems allow many users to access the system by maintaining an account of all the registered users.

Real-Time Operating System

They are intended for applications where data needs to be processed quickly, without any significant delays. For example, an anti-aircraft missile system must fire as soon as it receives a signal from the enemy aircraft.

Batch Processing Systems

In a batch processing system, similar jobs are clubbed together and submitted as a block to the processor for execution. User intervention is minimal in such systems. The jobs are picked up one by one and executed.

Language Processors, Device Drivers, Utilities & Application Software

Language Processor — it is a system software that translates the source code into an object code and vice-versa.

  • A Source Code refers to the program-code written in a high-level programming language (English-like words) such as C, Java, C++.
  • An Object Code is a code in machine language (0’s and 1’s) that can be understood by the computer.

Language processor can be of three types:

🔤 Assembler

Assembler converts a program written in Assembly Language (codes and mnemonics) to Machine Language and vice-versa.

🛠️ Compiler

Three categories of communication in a computer network

➡️ Simplex mode

In simplex mode, information can be transferred only in one direction. This mode is termed unidirectional.

A transmission medium refers to the channel of transmission through which data can be transmitted from one node to another in the form of a signal. A signal encodes the data in a form suitable for transmission on the medium.

Network Devices

There are various network devices, each of which plays a specific role in the network.

🔁 Repeater

A repeater is a network device which is used to restore (reshape and amplify) the input signal to its original form so that it can travel a larger distance. It is also known as a digital generator. It is placed between two cable segments.

🎯 Hub

A hub is a central connection networking device that connects single nodes to form a network. It transmits data in the form of binary bits to all the other nodes in the network.

🧿 Switch

A switch is a central connection networking device that connects single node/segments of LAN to form a network. It transmits data in the form of frames only to the destination node in the network.

Similarity between Hub and Switch: all the nodes connected through a hub/switch form only one LAN.

📠 Modem

The word "modem" stands for modulator-demodulator. A modem is used to send digital data over the phone line. It is an electronic device that converts Digital to analog signals (Modulation by the sending modem) and analog to Digital signals (Demodulation by the receiving modem) to transmit information over the network.

🌉 Bridge

A bridge is a multiport device used for connecting two or more local area networks (LAN), possibly operating at different speeds. Thus, a bridge may be used to produce a bigger LAN by combining smaller LANs.

🧭 Router

Routers are used for connecting various networks (LAN or WAN) with each other. A router maintains a routing table of various networks. It transmits data in the form of packets from an incoming network to the destination address in another network.

🚪 Gateway

A gateway connects networks based on different protocol technologies to communicate with each other. Data coming from one network operating on one protocol is converted according to the protocol of the outgoing network, and then forwarded. A gateway is like a router equipped with software for protocol conversion.

Topology & Bus Topology

Topology: The geometric arrangement (also called layout) of nodes in a network is called network topology.

Bus topology: In bus topology, there is a long cable, called a backbone cable (or simply backbone), that connects various nodes through a connector called a tap. In bus topology, a message sent by one is received by all devices connected to the backbone cable. This topology requires less cabling and is easy to install and extend the network laid using it. A terminator is a device that is used at the end of the backbone cable to absorb signals to avoid signal reflection.

✅ Advantages

  • Requires less cabling.
  • Easy to install and to extend.

⛔ Limitation

  • Backbone cable failure leads to disruption of the entire network.
  • Fault detection and isolation is difficult.

Star Topology

In star topology, all the devices are connected to the central controller called hub/switch through which the communication takes place.

  • If a hub is used, the messages are relayed to all the nodes in the network.
  • If a switch is used, the messages are sent only to the destination node.

A star network can be easily installed and configured. Fault detection and isolation is easy.

✅ Advantages

  • Easy to install and configure.
  • Fault detection and isolation is easy.

⛔ Limitations

  • It requires more cabling as compared to bus and ring topology.
  • Hub/Switch failure will lead to network failure.

Ring Topology

In ring topology, all the devices are attached through a cable in the form of a ring. The message to be communicated is transmitted in one direction, thereby relaying the message to the intended recipient. Addition/deletion of devices, fault detection and isolation is easy.

✅ Advantages

  • Addition/deletion of devices is easy.
  • Fault detection and isolation is easy.

⛔ Limitation

  • Single point failure leads to disruption of the entire network.

Tree Topology

Tree topology is a hybrid topology using a combination of star and bus topology. The backbone cable of a bus topology acts like the stem of the tree, and individual nodes/star networks are connected to the main backbone cable like the branches of a tree. Damage to a segment of a network laid using tree topology will not affect other segments.

✅ Advantage

  • Damage to one segment of the network does not affect the other segments.

⛔ Limitations

  • Installation and configuration are difficult as compared to other topologies.
  • Also, if the backbone cable is damaged, the entire network communication is disrupted.

Mesh Topology

In mesh topology, every node is connected to every other node in the network. Because of the dedicated point-to-point connection between every possible pair of nodes, the topology provides secure data transfer without any traffic problem.

✅ Advantages

  • Dedicated point-to-point links between every pair of nodes.
  • Secure data transfer without any traffic problem.

⛔ Limitation

  • It requires a large number of connections to be established in the topology. This leads to difficulty in installation as the number of nodes increases.

🧮 Useful for numericals: for n nodes, a full mesh needs n(n−1)/2 direct links and n(n−1) ports. So 5 nodes → 10 links, 6 nodes → 15 links.

To identify computers and users over the network

It is possible to identify computers and users over the network using MAC and IP address.

MAC address

A MAC (Media Access Control) address or Physical address is a unique 12-digit hexadecimal number assigned to each NIC by the manufacturer. The MAC address of an NIC is permanent and does not change.

By convention, MAC addresses are usually written in one of the following two formats:
MM:MM:MM:SS:SS:SS  OR  MM-MM-MM-SS-SS-SS

The first half (MM:MM:MM) of a MAC address contains the ID number of the adapter (NIC) manufacturer. The second half (SS:SS:SS) of a MAC address represents the serial number assigned to the adapter (NIC) by its manufacturer.

Eg: 00:A0:C9:14:C8:35 — 00:A0:C9 = ID number of the NIC manufacturer · 14:C8:35 = serial number assigned to the NIC by its manufacturer.

IP Address

An IP address is a group of four bytes (or 32 bits) number, each of which can be a number from 0 to 255.

Eg: 59.177.134.72

IP addresses are normally expressed in decimal format as a "dotted decimal number". IP protocol identifies a machine with its IP address to route the packets in a network. The IP address assigned to the PC may change when it connects to the network next time.

Database commands — CREATE · USE · SHOW · DROP

✔ Create Database

Create Database command creates a database, which acts as a container of tables.

CREATE DATABASE [IF NOT EXISTS] <database name>;

-- Example
CREATE DATABASE IF NOT EXISTS student;

The IF NOT EXISTS clause, if used, will first test whether a database by the mentioned name already exists or not. If it does, then the create database command is simply ignored, otherwise a database with the mentioned name is created.

✔ Open Database

The USE statement is used to select/open an already existing database on which the operations can be performed.

USE <database name>;

-- Example
USE student;
✔ Show Database

The SHOW DATABASES command displays the names of existing databases. To display specific databases, use the LIKE clause.

SHOW DATABASES;
SHOW DATABASES LIKE <database name>;

-- Example
SHOW DATABASES LIKE ‘student’;
✔ Remove Database

DROP DATABASE is used to drop an existing database. It removes the database along with its contents.

DROP DATABASE <database name>;

-- Example
DROP DATABASE student;

Tables — CREATE, constraints, DESCRIBE, SHOW TABLES, DROP

Creating Tables: ‘CREATE TABLE’ command is used to create a table. It defines the structure of the table that includes — field names, data type, size and constraint as per the requirements.

CREATE TABLE <table name> (
  <column name> datatype [(<size>)], <column name> datatype [(<size>)], …
);

-- To create a student table with schema  student (sno, name, dob)
CREATE TABLE student(sno int(2), name varchar(10), dob date);

⚠️ It is necessary to create a database or open an already existing database before issuing the CREATE TABLE command.

Constraints: Constraints are conditions that are applied to a column or a set of columns in a table. Constraints maintain data integrity and are also known as integrity constraints.

DESCRIBE Table command allows the user to view the structure of an already created table.

-- Example
DESCRIBE student;   Or   DESC student;
✔ View tables in the database

Show tables command displays all the tables of the current database.

SHOW TABLES;
✔ Drop Table

This statement is used to drop an existing table. When you use this statement, complete information present in the table will be lost.

DROP TABLE <table name>;

-- Example
DROP TABLE student;

Inserting Data in Tables

INSERT INTO command is used to insert new records into the table.

Syntax 1 — selected columns

The following syntax allows you to insert a new record with values on selected columns.

INSERT INTO <table name> [(<column list>)] VALUES (<value1>, <value2>, ……, <valuen>);

-- Schema: student (sno, name, address, phone, email)
INSERT INTO student(sno, name, phone) VALUES (1,’Arun’,7864532);
Syntax 2 — all columns

The following syntax allows you to insert a new record with values on all the columns.

INSERT INTO <table name> VALUES (<value1>, <value2>, …, <valuen>);

-- Schema: student (sno, name, address, phone, email)
INSERT INTO student VALUES (1,’Arun’, ‘P.BOX.NO.14, POSTAL CODE-516, Ibri’, 7864532, ’[email protected]’);
To insert NULL

To insert NULL in a specific column, the word NULL can be inserted without quotes in the given column. NULL value indicates missing/unknown/not applicable value.

INSERT INTO student VALUES (1,’Arun’, ‘P.BOX.NO.14, POSTAL CODE-516, Ibri’, 7864532, NULL);
To insert a Date

Date should be entered in the given format such as ‘YYYY-MM-DD’.

-- Schema: student (sno, name, address, phone, dob)
INSERT INTO student VALUES (1,’Arun’, ‘P.BOX.NO.14, POSTAL CODE-516, Ibri’, 7864532, ’1988-11-01’);

Modify the structure of the table — ALTER TABLE

ALTER TABLE command alters the structure of the table. It allows the user to add / delete / modify columns or constraints of the table.

ALTER TABLE <table_name> ADD/DROP <column_name> [datatype];
ALTER TABLE <table> MODIFY <column> <new_definition>;

➕ Add a column

To add a column Games to the table Student:

ALTER TABLE Student ADD Games VARCHAR(20);

🔁 Change a column

To change the newly added Games column to hold integers (in place of character data):

ALTER TABLE Student MODIFY games INTEGER;

➖ Delete a column

To delete a column of a table the ALTER TABLE statement is used with the DROP clause:

ALTER TABLE Student DROP Games;

🧠 Add = new column · Modify = change definition/datatype · Drop = remove a column · (Rename = the CHANGE clause) — ALTER never touches the rows, only the structure.

The SELECT command — the complete toolkit

Select command is used to retrieve a subset of rows or columns from one or more tables. In other words, it is used to make queries on the database.

SELECT <column name> [,<column name>,…..] FROM <table name> WHERE <condition>;

-- Example
SELECT sno, sname FROM student;
➊ To select / display all columns

In SELECT command, either a complete list of all the columns or “*” should be specified to display all the columns in a table.

-- Query to display all the columns of salesman table
SELECT * FROM salesman;
-- or
SELECT salesman_id, name, city, commission FROM salesman;
➋ To select required columns

In SELECT command, the column list of the required columns should be specified to display the required columns’ information.

-- Query to display name and commission of salesman table
SELECT name, commission FROM salesman;
➌ To reorder columns in the query result

SELECT command retrieves information based on the order of the columns specified by the user.

SELECT city, name, commission, salesman_id FROM salesman;
➍ To eliminate redundant data — DISTINCT

The DISTINCT keyword eliminates duplicate rows from the result of a SELECT statement.

SELECT DISTINCT(commission) FROM salesman;
➎ To select data from all the rows — ALL

The ALL keyword retains duplicate rows from the result of a SELECT statement. By default, SELECT command retains duplicate rows even if ALL is not specified.

SELECT ALL(commission) FROM salesman;
➏ To perform simple calculations

SELECT statement can be used to perform calculations and display the output. It can be used without the FROM clause as the computed values have no reference to any table.

SELECT 5+6;        -- Output: 11

MySQL provides a dummy small worktable called Dual, which has just one row and one column. It can be used for obtaining calculation results and system date.

SELECT 4*3 FROM dual;   -- Output: 12

The current date can be obtained using the curdate() function with the SELECT command.

SELECT curdate();      -- Output: 2020-11-10
➐ To use scalar expressions with selected fields

SQL allows performing simple numeric computations by placing scalar expressions and constants along with the selected fields.

SELECT name, commission*100 FROM salesman;
-- Output columns:  name | commission*100
➑ To use column aliases

In a select query, the columns can be given different headings (column alias name) with the as keyword for output purposes. The column alias name having more than one word or special characters should be enclosed in quotation marks.

SELECT <column name> as [column alias], <column name> as [column alias]
FROM <tablename>;

-- Example
SELECT name as “salesman name”, city, commission*100 as com FROM salesman;
-- Output headings:  salesman name | city | com
➒ To put text in the query output

In a select query, it is possible to insert text, enclosed within quotation marks, for output purposes. Text is inserted with the column names as per the requirement. The text makes the output more presentable, and it gets printed with every row of the output, not simply once for the table.

SELECT salesman_name, ’gets the commission’, comm.*100, ’%’ FROM salesman;

The WHERE clause — operators, BETWEEN, IN, LIKE, NULL

To select specific rows: The WHERE clause in the SELECT statement specifies the condition/criteria for selection of rows to be returned.

SELECT <column names> FROM <table name> WHERE <condition>;

-- Schema: student(name, dob, aggregate)
SELECT name, aggregate FROM student WHERE aggregate > 350;

🧮 Arithmetic operators

Used for calculations in an expression.

  • + addition
  • − subtraction
  • * multiplication
  • / division
  • % modulus (remainder)

⚖️ Relational operators

Relational operators compare two values and give a result in the form of True or False. An expression (combination of operands and operators) formed using a relational operator is called a condition (5<6). Relational expressions/conditions are used in the WHERE clause to filter records that satisfy the condition.

  • = equal to
  • > greater than · < less than
  • >= greater than or equal to · <= less than or equal to
  • <> or != not equal to

🔀 Logical operators

To combine two or more conditions, logical operators are used. The result of the logical expression is true or false.

  • OR (||) – Returns True only if any one or both the conditions are true.
  • AND (&&) – Returns True only if both the conditions are true.
  • NOT (!) – Returns True if the condition is False and vice-versa.
-- Schema: employee(empno, ename, grade)
SELECT empno, ename, grade FROM employee
WHERE (grade = 'E2' OR grade = 'E3');

Example for relational operator: Schema — suppliers(supplier no, name, city). Query to list all the members not from DELHI:

SELECT * FROM suppliers WHERE city <> ‘Delhi’
📏 Condition based on a range — BETWEEN

The BETWEEN operator defines a range of values that the column values must fall in, to make the condition True. The range includes both the lower value and the upper value. The operator NOT BETWEEN is the reverse of BETWEEN operator, that is, the rows not satisfying the BETWEEN condition are retrieved.

SELECT <column name> FROM <table name>
WHERE <column name> BETWEEN <lower value> AND <upper value>;

-- Query to list the items whose QOH falls between 30 to 50 (both inclusive)
SELECT Icode, Descp, QOH FROM Items WHERE QOH BETWEEN 30 AND 50;
📋 Condition based on a list — IN

IN operator is used to compare the given column against the specified list of values. It returns the record that matches a value in the given list of values. The NOT IN operator displays rows that do not match in the list.

SELECT <column names> FROM <table name>
WHERE <column name> IN (<value1>,<value2>,….<valuen>);

-- Schema: student (sname, city)
-- Query to return students from city ‘Delhi’, ‘Mumbai’ or ‘Kolkata’
SELECT * FROM student WHERE city IN (‘Delhi’,‘Mumbai’,‘Kolkata’);
🔍 Condition based on pattern matches — LIKE

LIKE is a string-matching operator that is used to compare character strings using patterns. Patterns are described using two special wildcard characters:
percent (%) – matches any substring.
underscore (_) – matches any single character.
Patterns are case-sensitive, that is, upper-case characters do not match lower-case characters, or vice-versa.

To illustrate pattern matching, consider the following examples:
• San% matches any string beginning with 'San'
• %idge% matches any string containing 'idge' as a substring, for example ‘Ridge’, ‘Bridges’, ‘Cartridge’, ‘Ridgeway’ etc.

SELECT <column names> FROM <table name> WHERE <column name> LIKE ‘<pattern string>’;

-- Schema: student (sname, marks, phoneno)
-- student name beginning with A as the first character
SELECT * FROM student WHERE sname LIKE ‘A%’;
-- student name ending with s as the last character
SELECT name FROM student WHERE name LIKE ‘%s’;
-- student name with b as the second character
SELECT name FROM student WHERE name LIKE ‘_b%’;
🕳️ Searching for NULL

In a select query, the IS NULL operator is used in the WHERE clause to check for empty values (NULL values). It returns the records that contain NULL in the given column.

SELECT <column names> FROM <table name> WHERE <column name> IS NULL;

-- pet table
SELECT name, birth, death FROM pet WHERE death IS NULL;
✅ Searching for NOT NULL

In a select query, the IS NOT NULL operator is used in the WHERE clause to check for values other than NULL. It returns the records that contain values other than NULL in the given column.

SELECT <column names> FROM <table name> WHERE <column name> IS NOT NULL;

-- pet table
SELECT name, birth, death FROM pet WHERE death IS NOT NULL;

Operator Precedence & Sorting results (ORDER BY)

Operator Precedence is the order in which the database evaluates different operators in the given expression.

Rules applied in evaluating an expression with multiple operators:

  • Operators with higher precedence get evaluated before operators of lower precedence.
  • Operators with equal precedence are evaluated from left to right within an expression.
  • In case of parentheses inside an expression, then it gets evaluated first.

UPDATE and DELETE — changing and removing records

✏️ Update command

Update command is used to modify the values of one or more records in the table.

UPDATE <tablename>
SET Column1 = Value1 [, Column2 = Value2, ..]
WHERE <Condition>;

The WHERE clause in an UPDATE command updates the selected records based on the condition. If omitted, ALL records will be updated!

-- Eg 1: To update English marks of ALL the students to 100 in student table
UPDATE student SET english=100;

-- Eg 2: To update english marks of Arun and David to 100
UPDATE student SET english=100 WHERE sname='Arun' or sname='David';
🗑️ Delete command

Delete command is used to delete one or more records from the given table. The WHERE clause in a DELETE query deletes selected records based on the condition; if omitted all the records would be deleted.

DELETE FROM <tablename> WHERE <Condition>;

-- Eg-1: To delete student records with sno < 12
DELETE FROM student WHERE sno<12;

-- Eg-2: To delete ALL the records of student table
DELETE FROM student;
Delete vs Drop vs Truncate — the classic viva question

DELETE … WHERE removes chosen rows · DELETE without WHERE empties the table but keeps its structure · DROP TABLE removes the rows and the structure — complete information present in the table will be lost.
